Privacy policy

Last revised: 10 October 2026

In one line

MultiDrive collects no data. There are no servers: the program runs on your own device, encrypts before uploading and talks directly to your own cloud accounts.

This is not a statement of intent, it is a consequence of how it is built. There is no MultiDrive infrastructure your data could reach even if we wanted it to. The code is free software (AGPL-3.0) and can be checked.

What it does with your files

  • It cuts them into chunks and encrypts them on your device with XChaCha20-Poly1305, using a key derived from a passphrase only you know.
  • It spreads those already-encrypted chunks across the cloud accounts you have configured.
  • Filenames are encrypted too: the provider sees neither the structure nor what anything is called.
  • The passphrase is not stored and does not leave the process. If you lose it, nobody —us included— can recover the volume.
  • With one exception, on Android and only if you turn it on: automatic folder upload needs the passphrase, because a job that starts on its own cannot ask you for it. When you turn that on, the passphrase is sealed in the Android keystore, encrypted by the phone's hardware and requiring the phone to be unlocked in order to be decrypted. It does not leave the device. The app tells you this before you turn it on, it ships turned off, and turning it off wipes the stored passphrase.

What it does with your Google account

When you link a Google Drive account, your browser opens on Google's own page. Your password never passes through MultiDrive: it goes from your browser to Google, and all that comes back is a single-use code exchanged for an access grant.

  • One single permission is requested: drive.file, which grants access only to the files MultiDrive creates. It cannot see, read or touch the rest of your Drive.
  • That permission is used exclusively to store, read and delete the volume's encrypted blocks. For nothing else.
  • The token that allows it to be refreshed is stored on your own device, in your user profile, with permissions that stop other users of the machine from reading it. It is not transmitted anywhere except to Google when refreshing.
  • On Android it lives in the app's private storage, which no other app can reach, and Android's automatic backup is disabled on purpose: if it were not, the system would have uploaded that file to your own Drive account by itself. Uninstalling the app deletes it.
  • You can withdraw the permission whenever you want from your Google account settings, or by removing the provider from MultiDrive's configuration.

MultiDrive's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Google data is not transferred to third parties, is not used for advertising and is not read by any human being.

What there isn't

  • No telemetry, no usage statistics, no automatic crash reports.
  • No MultiDrive accounts, no sign-up, no licence server.
  • No ads and no profiling, and nothing is sold or handed to third parties, because there is nothing to hand over.
  • This website uses no cookies and no analytics.

Who you talk to

MultiDrive communicates only with the storage providers you have configured and with their authentication services. That list is yours to decide, and the specific addresses are in the code, not hidden.

What those providers do with what you upload is governed by their policies, not by this one. What MultiDrive guarantees is that whatever arrives there was encrypted before it left your device.
